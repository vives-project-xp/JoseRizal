It was in 1891 when José Rizal found himself in Ghent. At the time, Rizal was deep into the writing and eventual publication of *El Filibusterismo* (*The Reign of Greed*)—his second novel, a sequel to *Noli Me Tangere*. He came to Ghent from Brussels, hoping to find a more affordable city where he could finalize and publish his manuscript, as he was struggling financially.

Rizal stayed in No. 37 Rue Philippe de Champagne (in Dutch, Filip van Arteveldestraat), a modest residence in a quiet part of the city. While Ghent was not a major hub for political exiles, it provided the calm and anonymity Rizal needed to focus on his work. The printing press he used was F. Meyer-Van Loo, located at 20 Heilige Geeststraat (Holy Ghost Street), just a short walk from his boarding house.
